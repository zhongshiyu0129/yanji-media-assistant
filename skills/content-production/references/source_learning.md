# Source Learning

Goal: study the original script before rewriting. Extract what is worth learning without copying protected or distinctive expression.

This stage is mandatory. Do it before correction, hook rewriting, or dedup rewriting.

## Extract

### Gold lines

Sentences with strong rhythm, memorable phrasing, contrast, or punch.

### Emotional lines

Sentences that create surprise, awe, warmth, historical feeling, family-country feeling, or ordinary-life resonance.

### Suspense devices

How the original makes the audience want to continue:

- Impossible fact.
- Scale comparison.
- "You thought X, but actually Y."
- Mystery object or strange phenomenon.
- Future reveal.

### Visual metaphors

Concrete images that make the topic visible:

- Distance comparison.
- Size comparison.
- Throwing a mountain into water.
- A lake as an ocean embryo.
- A desert wearing a grid coat.

### Data hooks

Numbers that can create scale, if verifiable.

### Techniques to learn

Describe the technique, not just the sentence:

- Start with an unbelievable fact.
- Immediately deny the simple explanation.
- Use scale comparison to make numbers visible.
- Use personification to make natural systems feel alive.
- Stack facts in escalating order.

## Do not

- Do not copy full original gold lines into the final script.
- Do not keep the same sequence of surprises.
- Do not keep unverifiable numbers without review.
- Do not flatten emotional force in the name of rigor.
